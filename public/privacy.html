Privacy

What we hold, why, and for how long. The short version: an email address and a licence record, and nothing else we could avoid.

Last updated 14 November 2026

What never reaches us at all

  • icr-merge and git-clash run entirely on your machine. They make no network requests and send us nothing — including no telemetry, no usage counts and no error reports.
  • Your signing identity for provenance is a keypair generated on your machine. The private key never leaves it, and there is no account database it could be registered against — author identity is the public key's own fingerprint.
  • Files your .gitignore excludes are never synced, and neither are .env files, private keys, certificates or your local identity file, regardless of what your ignore rules say.
  • The GitHub watcher (hivecode-mcp github) runs on your machine and reaches GitHub through your own gh login. Your GitHub credentials never reach us. The issues, comments and CI logs it reads go to your agent on your machine, and into the room that agent works in.
  • Notifications go from your machine straight to the Slack or Discord webhook you give them. We never see that webhook, and a room's link is never put in a notification.
  • The Slack bridge you run yourself (hivecode-mcp slack start) talks to Slack from your machine with your own app's tokens. Those tokens never reach us. Connecting a room through a Slack link is different: see Slack, when you connect a room to a channel below.

What we store

  • Your email address and licence record — plan, seat allowance, status, and the Razorpay payment or subscription id — held in Supabase so the relay can check whether a key is valid. This is the only personal account data we keep.
  • Payment details are not ours. Razorpay processes the payment and we never see your card. What we receive back is an identifier and an amount.

We keep the licence record while your licence exists, and for as long afterwards as we need it for tax and accounting purposes. Ask us to delete it and we will, once it is no longer needed for those obligations.

What passes through and stays on the relay

A room's participants exchange document updates through the relay over an encrypted connection (wss://). On standard open rooms (room-…), the room link itself is the capability: anyone who holds the link can read and write that room's synced files and chat. On private rooms (hs_…), access is gated by signed tokens: the host's private key stays in ~/.hivecode/keys on the hosting machine, and invite tokens live locally in ~/.hivecode/tokens (never inside the synced folder). Folder-scoped invitations (--paths) are enforced per file subdocument so out-of-scope file contents never reach that participant's machine; shared room records on the root document — chat messages, tasks, claims, and the synced list of filenames — remain visible to everyone admitted to the room (and, if the invite lets them write, can be changed by them; read-only invites cannot).

On the hosted relay, snapshots of a room's synced files, chat, tasks and claims are written to the container's local disk (.hive-relay-data) so a room remains accessible from the browser Control Room when editors close and survives a relay process restart. Signed revocation lists for private rooms follow the same persistence rule when HIVE_PERSIST_DIR or an embedder store is configured; when a relay runs memory-only (/__hive/revocations reports durable: false), revoked invites stay blocked after a relay restart only when an honest participant reconnects and re-presents the signed revocation list. The hosted relay runs on a plan whose container disk is wiped on a redeploy or container replacement, and there is no self-serve room-delete command yet: when a redeploy wipes the container, synced files come back from participants' own folders if they rejoin, and chat and tasks return only if a still-running client reconnects.

Hivecode's application logs record connection and channel events: the room id, when a participant joined and left, how long they stayed, how many participants were in the room, sample synced relative file paths (up to three per multiplexed burst, or per file when full audit logging is enabled), and the display name sent on connect. Hivecode's own application logs never record file contents or client IP addresses (Render's edge proxy terminates TLS under Render's standard platform request handling).

In the VS Code extension, sharing your display name with the relay can be switched off under Settings → Hivecode → Share Name With Relay (with it off, the extension connects anonymously). Agents connecting through hivecode-mcp send their room display name so activity and limits remain attributable per agent. Names are self-declared on open rooms — the relay has no user account system.

If none of that is acceptable for your code, run the relay yourself (npx hivecode-relay) and pin your machine to it with HIVE_RELAY_PIN or HIVE_PRIVATE=1. It is the same program, it is public, and then nothing about your work touches our infrastructure.

Slack, when you connect a room to a channel

hivecode-mcp slack connect links one room to one Slack channel, and the hosted relay then carries messages both ways itself. Nothing passes until someone in your Slack workspace approves the Hivecode app and picks the channel on Slack's own screen. On private rooms (hs_…), only the host or a maintainer the host invited can run slack connect, slack list, or slack disconnect, and the room is notified when a Slack channel is attached.

  • From Slack to the room. Slack sends the relay the messages posted in channels the Hivecode bot is a member of; the bot joins only the channel you picked. Messages people post in that channel are written into the room's chat under their Slack username (the relay asks Slack for it, which is why the app may read basic profile information). From then on they are part of the room like any other chat line: anyone who holds the room grant can read them, and they are kept with the room's snapshot described above. Messages from bots, and events for any other channel, are dropped; on private rooms (hs_…), messages from Slack guests (single- or multi-channel), Slack Connect strangers, bots, deactivated accounts, and people from another workspace are ignored (fail-closed if Slack user lookup fails; lookups are cached in memory for 5 minutes per connection, so a person demoted to guest or deactivated in Slack can still post for up to 5 minutes).
  • From the room to Slack. Every new chat line in the room, from people and agents alike, is posted to that channel by the bot, where every member of the channel can read it and your workspace's own Slack retention applies. Before a line is sent, full room join links (including any embedded invite token), room IDs (both room-… and hs_…), and anything that looks like an API key or token are scrubbed before posting to Slack or external error/webhook outputs. History from before the connection is not sent.
  • What the relay keeps. For each connection: the room id, the Slack workspace's id and name, the channel's id and name, the bot's user id, and the bot's access token. The token is encrypted (AES-256-GCM) on the relay's disk, never logged and never written into a room. The incoming-webhook address Slack returns during the install is discarded at once. Usernames looked up from Slack are held in memory only. The relay's logs record Slack errors, never message text or tokens.
  • How it ends. Remove the bot from the channel, uninstall the app from your workspace, or run slack disconnect: the stored token is deleted and nothing more passes. A connection that was started but never approved is deleted after 24 hours. Like room snapshots, stored connections are also lost when the hosted relay is redeployed (see above); the room then stops reaching Slack until it is connected again.
  • If you connect with your own Slack app instead (slack connect with a bot token and signing secret), the relay keeps that app's signing secret as well, encrypted in the same way, and everything else above is the same.

Third parties we use

  • Razorpay — payments.
  • Supabase — the licence database.
  • Vercel — this website and the licence API.
  • Render — the hosted relay.
  • Slack — only if you connect a room to a Slack channel (above).
  • Resend — sending your licence key, if email delivery is configured.
  • Google Fonts — the two typefaces on this site, which means Google receives a request from your browser when you load it.

What we do not do

No advertising, no analytics, no tracking pixels, no cookies set by us, and no selling or sharing of your data with anyone not listed above. The website sets no cookies at all; the Control Room stores the name you type in your own browser's local storage so you do not have to retype it, and that never leaves your device.

Your rights

Ask us what account or licence record we hold about you, ask us to correct it, or ask us to delete it: contact us and a person will answer. Room snapshots on the hosted relay have no individual delete command today and are cleared when the relay container is redeployed or replaced; for immediate local control over room storage and deletion, run the relay on your own machine or network. If we ever suffer a breach that affects your data, we will tell you.
